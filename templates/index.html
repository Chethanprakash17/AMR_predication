Welcome to the AMR Risk Prediction System

This website demonstrates an end-to-end AI workflow for predicting the risk of antimicrobial resistance (AMR) in Intensive Care Unit (ICU) patients using early Electronic Health Record (EHR) data collected during ICU admission.

⚠ Academic Research Prototype This system is a research prototype developed for academic purposes only and is not intended for real clinical use. All predictions are for demonstration and educational evaluation as part of the Experiential Learning (EL) project.

The Problem: AMR in ICU Settings

Antimicrobial resistance (AMR) is a critical global health threat, particularly dangerous in ICU settings where critically ill patients are highly vulnerable to resistant infections. Traditional microbiology culture tests require 48–72 hours to identify resistant pathogens, creating a diagnostic delay that can compromise patient outcomes.

Our AI-based system provides an early probability-based AMR risk score using EHR data collected during ICU admission, potentially supporting clinicians in making more informed early treatment decisions while awaiting confirmatory culture results.

🎯 Our Goal

Develop a machine learning model to predict AMR risk early in ICU admission using readily available EHR data.

📊 Data Source

MIMIC-IV ICU Clinical Database containing comprehensive EHR data from ~30,000-40,000 ICU admissions.

🤖 Approach

XGBoost classifier trained on demographics, vitals, labs, and comorbidities from the EHR data collected during ICU admission.

System Workflow Overview

Figure 1: End-to-end workflow from EHR data collection to AMR risk score generation

The system operates in four stages:

  1. EHR Data Collection: Gather patient data during the inital hours after ICU admission
  2. Feature Engineering: Extract and process demographics, vitals, labs, and comorbidities
  3. ML Model: XGBoost classifier processes features to calculate risk
  4. AMR Risk Score: Output probability score indicating likelihood of antimicrobial resistance

System Scope & Limitations

✓ What This System DOES

  • Provides an early AMR risk probability score
  • Supports clinical decision-making
  • Offers transparent, explainable predictions

✗ What This System Does NOT Do

  • ✗ Prescribe antibiotics
  • ✗ Replace doctors or clinical judgment
  • ✗ Make diagnostic or treatment decisions
Clinical Context: The final clinical decision always remains with the attending physician. This system is intended to augment, not replace, existing clinical workflows.
