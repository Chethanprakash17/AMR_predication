Model Explainability & Transparency

Understanding why a model makes certain predictions is crucial for clinical trust and adoption. This page provides insights into which features contribute most to AMR risk predictions and how the model makes its decisions.

Why Explainability Matters: Clinicians need to understand the reasoning behind AI predictions to make informed decisions. Black-box models that provide scores without explanation are difficult to trust and integrate into clinical workflows.

Feature Importance Analysis

The XGBoost model provides feature importance scores that indicate how much each feature contributes to the prediction. Higher scores mean that feature has more influence on the model's decisions.

Figure 1: Top 10 most important features for AMR risk prediction

Key Findings

The most influential factors in predicting AMR risk are:

1. Prior Antibiotic Exposure

Importance: 0.18

Patients with recent antibiotic use have significantly higher AMR risk. Antibiotic exposure creates selective pressure that promotes resistant bacterial strains.

2. Broad-Spectrum Antibiotic Use

Importance: 0.15

Use of broad-spectrum antibiotics (e.g., carbapenems, vancomycin) in the past 90 days is strongly associated with AMR development.

3. Age

Importance: 0.12

Older patients (≥65 years) show increased AMR risk, likely due to cumulative healthcare exposure, comorbidities, and age-related immune changes.

4. Mechanical Ventilation

Importance: 0.11

Patients requiring mechanical ventilation are at higher risk for ventilator-associated pneumonia (VAP) caused by resistant organisms.

5. ICU Length of Stay

Importance: 0.10

Longer ICU stays increase exposure to nosocomial (hospital-acquired) resistant pathogens.

6. Lactate Level

Importance: 0.09

Elevated lactate indicates sepsis severity. Patients with severe sepsis often have more aggressive infections, including resistant organisms.

Clinical Interpretation of Feature Contributions

Antibiotic History (Most Important)

The model consistently prioritizes antibiotic exposure history as the strongest predictor. This aligns with clinical evidence that prior antibiotic use is the most significant modifiable risk factor for AMR development. Key mechanisms include:

  • Selective Pressure: Antibiotics kill susceptible bacteria, allowing resistant strains to proliferate
  • Microbiome Disruption: Broad-spectrum antibiotics disrupt normal flora, reducing colonization resistance
  • Persistent Colonization: Resistant organisms can persist in the gut for months after antibiotic exposure

Severity Indicators

Features like mechanical ventilation, lactate levels, and WBC count reflect illness severity. Severely ill patients are more likely to:

  • Have aggressive infections requiring prolonged hospitalization
  • Require invasive devices that serve as bacterial entry points
  • Have compromised immune function making them vulnerable to resistant pathogens

Comorbidities

Chronic conditions like diabetes, CKD, and immunosuppression contribute to AMR risk through:

  • Frequent healthcare encounters and antibiotic exposure
  • Impaired immune response to infections
  • Chronic wounds (diabetes) that harbor resistant organisms

Building Clinical Trust Through Transparency

For AI systems to be adopted in healthcare, clinicians must understand and trust their predictions. Explainability helps achieve this through:

🔍 Clinical Validation

Feature importance aligns with known AMR risk factors from clinical literature

📚 Educational Value

Reinforces evidence-based understanding of AMR drivers among healthcare providers

⚡ Debugging & Auditing

Allows identification of potential biases or errors in model reasoning

🤝 Shared Decision-Making

Enables clinicians to incorporate AI insights alongside their clinical judgment

Best Practice: Always present model predictions alongside explanations of contributing factors. This empowers clinicians to critically evaluate the model's reasoning and determine if it makes clinical sense for each specific patient.

Case: AMR Risk Score = 72% (High Risk)

Patient Characteristics:

  • 78-year-old male
  • Emergency ICU admission with septic shock
  • Recent hospitalization with broad-spectrum antibiotic use (30 days ago)
  • Comorbidities: Diabetes, CKD Stage 3
  • Requiring mechanical ventilation
  • Elevated lactate (4.2 mmol/L), elevated WBC (18.5 ×10³/µL)

Top Contributing Factors:

  1. Recent broad-spectrum antibiotic use - Highest contributor
  2. Mechanical ventilation - Invasive device risk
  3. Age > 65 - Increased vulnerability
  4. Elevated lactate - Sepsis severity indicator
  5. Diabetes + CKD - Multiple comorbidities

Clinical Recommendation:

This patient's high AMR risk score, driven primarily by recent antibiotic exposure and severity indicators, may warrant empirical broad-spectrum therapy covering resistant gram-negative organisms while awaiting culture results.
